## PART 3 — Discovery-listening guide (NOT a cold call)

Warm conversations to learn, never to sell. In person, over coffee, in writing, or as a LinkedIn message. Fully Zen: listening, not pitching.

**Opening:** "I'm mapping how small [engineering/fiduciary] teams actually spend their week — not selling anything, just understanding the real bottlenecks. Could I ask you a few questions about how yours runs?"

**Five questions:**
1. Walk me through a normal week — where does your team's time actually go?
2. Which recurring task feels most repetitive or annoying?
3. When something comes in — an inquiry, an invoice, a request — what happens, step by step?
4. Where do things slip through, get missed, or get redone?
5. Have you tried any tools for this? What worked, and what put you off?

**Close (no ask):** "Genuinely helpful — thank you. Mind if I follow up if I come across something that fits?"

Listen for a task that is repetitive, predictably triggered, time-eating, and measurable if automated. That's a wedge, confirmed from the field.

### POST B — the differentiator (the "API gap," reworked: API-first, human-gated, reach where APIs don't)

*Image: optional simple "app A ✕ app B — no plug" diagram. Link: none.*

A quiet reason automation "doesn't work" for a lot of Swiss SMEs — and what actually gets past it.
Most automation connects software through APIs — the digital plug that lets two systems talk. When a system has a clean API, we use it. That's the fast, reliable path, and it runs most of what we build.
But much of the software Swiss businesses actually live in has no open plug, or a gated, costly one. So the standard "connect app A to app B" approach hits a wall exactly where the pain is worst.
Two ways past that wall: expensive certified middleware, or AI that operates the software the way a person would — with a human checking each step.
We do both sides: clean APIs where they exist, interface-level reach where they don't. And in every case a person approves before anything is saved or sent — because one wrong entry in accounting isn't a rounding error, it's a problem.
Less flashy than "fully autonomous." Also the part that actually holds up.
If you've been told your systems "can't be automated because they don't talk to each other" — that's often not the end of the story. Just not the easy version of it.

### POST E — "Real vs fake audit" — NEW, spare pure-value post

*Image: none. Link: none. Publish any time as standalone value.*

Every week now, small businesses get emails offering to "fix" their website or their AI visibility. Some are real. Most aren't. Five tells that it's noise:

1. A precise scare number it can't possibly know — "you're losing 42% of your traffic." No one can produce that from the outside. A real audit shows you where it looked and what it found.
2. The reply-gate — "just answer 'Interested' and I'll send the details / price list." A real service tells you something useful up front; it doesn't hold the findings hostage to a reply.
3. No specifics, just "issues" — vague "major problems" with no example. Real feedback names the actual page, the actual gap.
4. Leaked template seams — a stray "[25]" after a job title, a broken link, your domain pasted three times. Mass-mail, not a person who looked.
5. No accountable name or verifiable company — a throwaway domain, no real person, no address. Real help is happy to be found and checked.

What a real audit looks like: specific findings you can verify yourself, a named person, a transparent method, and no invented percentages. If it pressures you to reply fast, that's marketing — not analysis.

## PART 5 — Site task (internal, NOT for posting): Make VortexDeep findable by AI (GEO)

An AI-automation firm should show up when a buyer asks Claude or ChatGPT for one. Most of this is content hygiene, not a new project. A weekend pass over your own site (and mgredswissline.ch):

1. **Test where you stand today.** In a fresh, logged-out chat, ask ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini: "Who offers human-in-the-loop AI automation for SMEs near Zürich?" Note whether VortexDeep appears, and who does. That's your baseline — repeat monthly.
2. **Make the entity unambiguous.** One clear, consistent statement of who you are, what you do, and where (Zürich / Switzerland / worldwide) — identical on the site, LinkedIn, and the Company Page. AI models build their picture from consistent signals across sources.
3. **Add citation-worthy substance.** AI engines prefer content with concrete figures, sources, and clear claims — adding statistics and quotable statements measurably lifts how often you're cited. Your Swiss ROI numbers and the API-gap explanation are exactly this; put them on the page as clean, quotable lines.
4. **Structure for machines.** Clear headings, plain Q&A blocks (your Fit Check questions are a natural FAQ), and schema.org structured data (Organization + FAQ). LLM crawlers reward readable structure.
5. **Confirm your build is crawlable.** Many AI bots don't run JavaScript — server-side-rendered / static HTML is what they read. Your `dist` static build is likely already fine; just confirm the real text is in the HTML, not injected by JS.
6. **Check the logs.** See whether GPTBot, ClaudeBot, and PerplexityBot are actually hitting the site. If they're not crawling you, nothing downstream matters.
7. **Earn a few off-site mentions.** A large share of brand mentions in AI answers comes from third-party sources, not your own site — so a directory listing, a partner page, or a guest mention does more than another homepage tweak.
